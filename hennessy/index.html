Hennessy Le Club

Repositioning a membership
center around the trip

Repositioning Hennessy's membership center from rewarding buyers to serving members through the whole trip. Store-page visits went up 15.2%.

01The Problem

Members stopped buying abroad, but the membership center was still built for buyers.

Hennessy Le Club rewards members for buying Hennessy at duty-free shops abroad. Then buying abroad stopped being cheaper, and duty-free sales almost stopped. Travel came back, but not to shop, so the product needed a new position:

  1. Business goal

    Ride the travel rebound: move from duty-free sales to brand exposure and a long-term relationship.

  2. Product weakness

    The membership center was built for buyers: no purchase, no reason to open it.

  3. Where the weakness hurts

    Perks felt dull, sat too deep, and never reached members on the trip.

02Our Question

How might we give members a reason to stay close to Hennessy that doesn't depend on buying?

The trip was the answer: at the airport, in the city, and back home. The membership center had to be useful at all three moments.

03Our Plan

Perks that turn a trip into Hennessy memories,
all in one membership center

We widened the perks: beyond purchase rewards, new ones create trip moments members remember with Hennessy (Decision #1). One membership center brings them all together and shows members where (#2) and when (#3) they need them.

With a fixed budget and stores in several countries to align, we started small: a 6-week pilot in Singapore for Chinese New Year 2024, with more cities planned next.

05Decision #2 | One home for perks

One home for perks cut the steps from 5 taps to 2.

Perks lived inside each store page. To see what they could get, members had to pick a city, then a store. Our first redesigns only reshuffled those levels, so the real question was who's looking, and for what.

Hennessy's member data answered it: three kinds of travellers, each after a different kind of perk, so one layout had to serve all three.

  • CelineForeign student

    Looks for deals she can browse at her own pace.

  • JamesBusiness traveler

    Looks for what's here: perks at this airport, right away.

  • JohnCognac lover

    Looks for experiences: tastings and keepsakes.

We tested three layouts against them; only a mixed home did. Point at a traveller to see their part.

Ideally the page would change for each member. For a 6-week MVP, one shared page was faster to build and easier to update when perks changed.

06Decision #3 | The right nudge

Pop-ups remind members at the right time and place.

I led this decision, from the location-permission flow to the pop-up rules and the onboarding and check-in flows.

The membership center can't help if members forget to open it. A welcome tour was easy; pop-ups were harder, because they need the member's location, which takes two separate permissions.

So pop-ups stay short:

  • Only the best perks: special travel perks and cognac tastings.
  • Only what helps now: the closest perk within 100 meters, or the city page if we only know the city.

We ask once in the app, with a clear reason. With location on, a pop-up can point to the perk nearby, which helped claimed perks get used 5.4% more during the pilot.

—What I Learned

Good design has to last, not just look nice.

1. Looks are not enough; cost matters too. My first hub gave each city its own custom illustration. In review, the team pointed out that every new city would then need a new drawing, which adds cost every time we expand. So we switched to a reusable city module, a landmark photo plus perk cards, which is what shipped.

2. Plans change, so keep adjusting. Every perk was tied to a real store, so each change needed sign-off across countries, and that took time. Cities and perks changed 3 times. Because each perk was its own card in the mixed home (Decision #2), each change meant swapping a card, not redesigning a page.
