Pawtner

Practice pet care
before you bring one home

A virtual pet that lets first-time owners try the daily care, the routine and the costs before they commit to a real animal.

Project
Passion Project
Timeline

Team
2 Product Designers, Incl. Me

01The Problem

People adopt on a feeling, and only find out if they're ready once the pet is home.

After moving to New York, far from family, I wanted a dog for company. Then I asked myself a question I couldn't answer: am I actually ready? I had never cared for a pet, and nothing I could read let me try it first.

  1. The stakes

    About 1 in 10 adopted pets are no longer in their new home six months later.*1

  2. What's missing

    There's no safe way to try pet care before the commitment is real.

  3. Where it hurts

    Cute videos show the fun part, and advice from guides, forums and friends is scattered and contradictory.

*1American Humane Association, Keeping Pets in Homes, Phase II, 2013.

02Our Question

How might we help potential pet owners understand what pet care really takes before they fully commit?

Reading wasn't working. People needed to feel the routine for themselves.

🤔 Which key decisions did we make?

We tested the first version with 6 users. Three screens changed the most.

04Decision #1 | Onboarding

Help people choose a pet before asking them to pick one.

Before

  • 3 of 6 “This screen doesn't tell me much about pet selection.”
  • 3 of 6 “What happens after? How long do I use the app for?”

After

  • Real breeds, grouped from beginner-friendly up, so the choice says something about the work.
  • Name your pet so it feels like yours from the start.
  • Pick a journey of 14, 30 or 60 days, so people know how long it lasts.

05Decision #2 | Home

Make the home screen feel like a home, not a dashboard.

Before

  • 4 of 6 didn't connect with the static dog.
  • 6 of 6 found the button interactions confusing.

After

  • The pet reacts. Its look changes with how you care for it.
  • Grouped features: status top left, your tools top right, care at the bottom.
  • Labelled buttons replace the icon row and the tab bar.

06Decision #3 | Pals

Give people a reason to come back: their friends' pets.

Before

  • 6 of 6 liked the idea of a friends page.
  • But the pet info was thin and there was nothing to do.

After

  • Live status and personality on every friend's pet; flip the card for details.
  • Play with friends' pets and go on a shared 15-minute walk.

07Final Design

23 days with Max.

Day One: So excited to meet you, Max!

Onboarding asks one question first: thinking of getting a pet? Then you bring a new pet home.

Day Three: Figuring out what you like

Feeding and learning. Max reminds you when it's time, even when the app is closed.

Day Ten: Walks are becoming our thing

Play and exercise become a routine, with a nudge when a walk is due.

Day Twenty: Max made a new friend today

Socializing. Visit friends' pets, check how they're doing and play together.

Day Twenty-Three: I think I'm ready

Reflecting. A readiness report shows your responsibility, patience and empathy.

08What's Next

Show the hard parts too, not just the cute ones.

A mentor asked us: does Pawtner prepare people for a real pet, or just make them feel ready? We leaned on fun and bonding, and left out the friction real owners face. Next:

1Lifecycle simulationsPuppy to senior, not just the first 30 days.
2Tough momentsA late night at work, a sudden vet bill, a pet that won't settle.
3A real-world transition toolFrom a virtual pet to fostering or adopting a real one.

—What I Learned

Approachable isn't the same as accurate.

1. Start from your own question. “Am I ready?” was my question first. That made the problem easy to explain and easy to test.

2. Small tests change big things. Six people were enough to show that the static dog, the icon buttons and the empty friends page didn't work.

3. A good simulation needs friction. Making care feel fun kept people playing, but readiness also means facing the hard parts.
