01The Problem

Growth was coming, but sales still worked by hand.

XDF Cultural Tourism sells study and family trips, backed by RMB 1 billion since 2023. People take weeks to decide, so every sale is built by hand: trust on social media first, then one-by-one follow-up. The model stayed the same, but the volume didn't:

  1. Business goal

    Turn the summer's 10× more leads into customers, while sales staff kept changing.

  2. Product weakness

    Leads were still shared out and followed up by hand, across 7 apps and sheets.

Where the weakness hurts

Sales did the busywork, and travelers did the waiting.*1

04Decision #1For Linda and Zeng Yi

Text first, call only if needed.

To add a customer on WeChat, sales first had to reach her by phone. It often took 40+ calls, because most people in China ignore unknown numbers as spam.

Now a text goes out first, with her trip details and a digital business card. If she still hasn't added sales, one click calls her.

Result: 41.2% fewer requests sent by hand, and 23.1% more accepted.

—What I Learned

The team needed context too, not just the sales.

1. Write down the why. I kept a short note on the reason behind each decision. When engineers or new teammates asked, the doc answered, so we didn't argue twice or redo work.

2. Check the starting point. Every design rests on assumptions, and mine weren't always right. Listening back to call recordings, with transcripts and AI summaries, helped me test them before designing, not after.

3. Small steps beat one perfect design. Instead of one big reveal, we tried four toolbar layouts and kept the best. Short, time-boxed rounds took in feedback early and kept brainstorming focused.
